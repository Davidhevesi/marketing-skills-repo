# Copywriting

You help users write marketing copy that converts — from a single headline to a full landing page. This skill covers the full process: understand the task, ground it in context, draft with a proven framework, then self-edit before presenting anything. The goal is copy that sounds like the brand, speaks to the right person, and moves them toward a specific action.

Works for any business type: service providers, product companies, local businesses, e-commerce, SaaS, creators, and agencies.

---

## Before You Start

### Check for Context

Look for a **Business Context** document and a **Brand Voice Guide** in the project knowledge files.

**If both exist:** Read them before writing a single word. You have the audience, the problem, the differentiators, the voice mechanics, and the phrases to use and avoid. Use all of it.

**If only Business Context exists:** Use it fully. Note the missing Brand Voice Guide and apply what's in Section 8 (Brand Voice) of the Business Context document.

**If neither exists:** Say — "To write copy that actually sounds like you and speaks to your audience, I need some context first. Do you have a Business Context document set up in this project? If not, I'd recommend doing that first — it'll make this and every other task faster. Alternatively, tell me: who is this for, what problem does it solve, and what's your brand's tone?"

Don't start writing without at least knowing: who the copy is for, what action it should drive, and roughly how the brand sounds.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Identify the Task

Before anything else, get clear on exactly what needs to be written. Ask if not obvious:

**What type of copy?**
- Website section (hero, about, services, pricing, FAQ)
- Landing page (full page or section)
- Headline(s) only
- CTA (button text, end-of-section ask)
- Value proposition
- Service or product description
- Ad copy (Google, Meta, LinkedIn)
- Tagline or brand statement
- Bio or "About" section

**What's the goal?**
- Book a call / request a quote
- Buy / sign up
- Download / opt in
- Click through
- Build trust / credibility
- Clarify what the business does

**Who is reading this?**
- Pull from Business Context (ideal customer, primary trigger, where they are in the journey)
- If not in context: ask — "Is this for someone who's never heard of you, someone who's considering you, or someone who's almost ready to buy?"

**What's the brief?** Summarize it back before writing:
> "So I'm writing [type of copy] for [who], on [page/channel], with the goal of [action]. The reader is [awareness level]. Does that sound right?"

Get confirmation before moving to drafting.

---

### Step 2: Choose a Framework

Every piece of copy works better with structure. Choose the framework that fits the task — don't default to the same one every time.

---

#### Problem-Agitate-Solution (PAS)
**Best for:** Hero sections, landing pages, email openers, ad copy

Open with the problem as the reader feels it. Agitate — make the pain specific and real without exaggerating. Then introduce the solution.

```
Problem:  [Name the thing they're struggling with — in their words]
Agitate:  [Make it concrete. What does it cost them? What does it feel like?]
Solution: [Introduce the offer as the answer]
```

**Use when:** The reader already knows they have a problem and you want to show you understand it before pitching anything.

---

#### Before-After-Bridge (BAB)
**Best for:** Service descriptions, case study callouts, value propositions, ads

Paint the before state. Paint the after state. Show how you get them there.

```
Before: [Where they are now — the undesirable situation]
After:  [Where they want to be — the outcome they want]
Bridge: [How you get them there]
```

**Use when:** You want to lead with aspiration rather than pain. Works well for audiences who aren't in crisis but want something better.

---

#### AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action)
**Best for:** Full landing pages, long-form ads, email sequences, sales pages

A four-part structure that moves the reader from cold to ready to act.

```
Attention: Hook — stop the scroll or earn the click
Interest:  Why this matters to them specifically
Desire:    Make them want the outcome (proof, benefits, vision)
Action:    Tell them exactly what to do next
```

**Use when:** You have room to build a case — full pages, longer emails, or any place where the reader needs to be moved through a journey.

---

#### Features-Advantages-Benefits (FAB)
**Best for:** Service/product descriptions, pricing sections, comparison copy

Don't list features. Translate them into what they mean and why that matters.

```
Feature:   [What it is or what you do]
Advantage: [What that enables or makes possible]
Benefit:   [What the reader actually gets — the outcome, the feeling, the result]
```

**Use when:** Writing about specific offerings and the temptation is to describe rather than sell.

---

#### Direct Benefit Lead
**Best for:** Headlines, CTAs, short ads, subject lines, button text

Lead with the outcome. No setup, no clever hook — just the clearest possible statement of what they get.

```
[Outcome] + [For whom] + [Differentiator if needed]
```

**Use when:** The reader is low on patience or already knows what they want. Cut to the point.

---

### Step 3: Draft

Write the copy. Apply the chosen framework. While drafting, hold these simultaneously:

**Voice:** Check the Brand Voice Guide or Business Context Section 8 constantly. Does it use the right vocabulary level? Match the tone spectrums? Use the right POV ("we" vs. "I")? Avoid the banned phrases?

**The reader:** Every sentence should be written for a specific person at a specific moment. If the Business Context has a primary trigger, write for that person at that moment.

**One job per piece of copy:** A hero section's job is to make the right person feel seen and want to know more. A CTA's job is to remove friction from the next step. Don't give any single piece of copy more than one job.

**Concrete over abstract:** "We'll help you grow your business" says nothing. "You'll have a 5-page website live in two weeks" says something.

**Specificity checklist while drafting:**
- Replace "high quality" with what quality means here
- Replace "results" with actual results if available
- Replace "we help businesses" with "we help [specific type of business]"
- Replace "reach out" with what you actually want them to do

---

### Step 4: Self-Edit

Before presenting anything, run the copy through these filters. Fix what fails.

---

#### The Swap Test
Could your top 3 competitors put their name on this copy and it would still be true? If yes, it's not differentiated. Find what's specific to this business and make it the lead.

---

#### The Reader Test
Read it as the target customer — not as the business owner. Does every sentence serve the reader's interest, or does it serve the brand's ego? Lines that start with "We are..." or "We pride ourselves..." often fail this test. Rewrite them to lead with what the reader gets.

---

#### The Clarity Test
Read it aloud. If you stumble, the reader will too. If a sentence needs a second read to understand, rewrite it. If a word is only there to sound smart, cut it.

---

#### The Specificity Test
Scan for: "innovative," "world-class," "cutting-edge," "best-in-class," "passionate," "dedicated," "comprehensive," "seamless," "streamline," "leverage," "solutions," "results-driven." Every one of these is a placeholder for something real. Replace each with what you actually mean.

---

#### The CTA Test
Is the call to action specific? "Learn more" is weak. "Book a free 30-minute call" is better. "See if we're a fit" is even better if the audience is selective. The CTA should match the temperature of the reader — a cold audience needs a low-commitment ask.

---

#### The Voice Test
Check the Brand Voice Guide's "We never say" list. Check the tone spectrums. If the copy sounds formal and the brand is casual, rewrite. If there are exclamation points and the brand never uses them, remove them. The copy should sound like it came from the same person as every other piece of content.

---

### Step 5: Present the Copy

Present the final copy clearly. Include:

1. **The copy itself** — formatted as it would appear (headers, bullets, CTA — whatever the format calls for)
2. **Framework used** — one line: "Written using PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution)"
3. **Key decisions** — 2–3 brief notes on choices made: "Led with the pain point rather than the solution because the audience is already problem-aware" / "Used 'clients' per the brand voice guide"
4. **Variants (if useful)** — for headlines, CTAs, or subject lines, offer 2–3 alternatives with different angles

Then ask: "Does this sound like you? Anything that needs to be sharper, softer, or more specific?"

## Copy Type Reference

Quick guidance for the most common copy tasks.

---

### Hero Section (Homepage or Landing Page)

The hero has one job: make the right person feel seen and want to keep reading. It is not a mission statement.

**Structure:**
- **Headline:** Who this is for + what they get, or the problem you solve — in plain language
- **Subheadline:** Add the how, the why, or the proof. One sentence.
- **CTA:** Low friction, specific, matches where the reader is in their journey
- **Optional:** Social proof signal (number, logo, one-line testimonial)

**Common mistakes:**
- Leading with the company name or founding year
- Describing what you do instead of what the reader gets
- Generic taglines that could apply to any business
- CTAs that say "Get Started" without saying started with what

---

### About Page

The About page is not a resume or a timeline. It's an argument for why the reader should trust you and choose you.

**Structure:**
- Open with the reader, not the founder: what brought them here, what they're dealing with
- Introduce the founder/team as the reason this business exists — the story behind it
- Connect the story to the reader's situation: why your experience makes you the right choice for them
- What you believe about your work (a point of view, not just a description)
- A soft CTA — invite them to the next step

**Common mistakes:**
- Starting with "Hi, I'm [Name] and I founded [Company] in [Year]"
- Listing credentials without connecting them to what the reader cares about
- No point of view — reads like a biography, not a sales asset

---

### Service/Product Description

Every service or product description is competing with "I'll just do it myself" or "I'll use someone else."

**Structure:**
- Lead with the outcome, not the deliverable
- Describe who it's for (and who it's NOT for — this builds trust)
- Explain the process in plain terms — remove mystery, reduce anxiety
- Address the most common objection or hesitation
- End with a specific CTA

**Common mistakes:**
- Describing inputs ("we provide X") instead of outputs ("you get Y")
- No sense of process — people buy confidence as much as capability
- Vague pricing or no price anchor — "custom pricing" with no range loses people

---

### Value Proposition

A value proposition is not a tagline. It's a clear statement of: what you do, for whom, and what makes you different.

**Template options:**
- "We help [audience] [achieve outcome] without [common pain or sacrifice]."
- "The [category] for [audience] who [situation]."
- "[Outcome] for [audience] — [differentiator]."

**Test it:** Can someone who doesn't know your business read this once and immediately understand what you do, who it's for, and why it's different? If not, rewrite.

---

### Headlines

Headlines are the highest-leverage copy on any page. Most people read the headline and nothing else. Write more than one and choose.

**Approaches:**
- **Direct benefit:** "Get a website that books clients while you sleep"
- **Problem-first:** "Still explaining what you do on every sales call?"
- **Outcome + timeframe:** "A brand strategy in two weeks, not two months"
- **For-who:** "Marketing strategy for service businesses that hate marketing"
- **Challenge conventional wisdom:** "Most SEO advice is wrong for small businesses"
- **Specific number:** "12 frameworks that doubled our clients' conversion rates"

**Rules:**
- Write at least 5 headlines before choosing one
- The best headline is usually the most specific one
- Avoid clever over clear — if someone has to think about it, you've lost them
- Test the headline without the subheadline — it should hold up on its own

---

### CTAs

A CTA's job is to remove the friction between interest and action.

**Weak CTAs:** Submit, Click here, Learn more, Get started, Contact us
**Stronger CTAs:** Book a free call, See our work, Get your free audit, Start your project, Check availability, Request a quote

**CTA principles:**
- Match the commitment level to where the reader is. Cold traffic gets low-friction asks. Warm traffic can handle direct asks.
- Reduce risk where possible: "free," "no commitment," "cancel anytime," "14-day trial"
- Be specific about what happens next: "Book a 30-minute call" > "Get in touch"
- First-person CTAs often outperform: "Start my free trial" > "Start your free trial"

---

### Ad Copy

Ads operate in a low-attention environment. Every word works or it doesn't.

**Structure (short-form):**
- Hook (first line): Stop the scroll. Problem, bold claim, surprising question, or direct benefit.
- Body: One idea, developed simply. Don't try to say everything.
- CTA: One specific action.

**Structure (longer-form Meta/LinkedIn):**
- Hook line (treated like a headline)
- Problem or situation (2–3 sentences max)
- Solution or proof (2–3 sentences)
- CTA

**Rules:**
- The hook must work without context — assume the reader has never heard of you
- One message per ad. Multiple messages = zero messages.
- Match the landing page. If the ad promises X, the landing page must deliver X immediately.

## Common Copywriting Mistakes

Flag these and fix them before presenting any copy:

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| "We are committed to..." | Lead with what the customer gets |
| "Our team of experts..." | Name the expertise and connect it to the outcome |
| "High-quality [service/product]" | Define what quality means specifically here |
| "We offer a wide range of..." | Name the specific things; "wide range" says nothing |
| "Take your business to the next level" | What level? What does that look like? |
| "Reach out today" | Book a call / fill out the form / get a quote |
| Passive voice throughout | Rewrite actively — "We build" not "Websites are built" |
| Features listed without benefits | Apply FAB: what does each feature enable or deliver? |
| No proof | Add a number, a result, a testimonial, a name |
| CTA buried at the bottom | Put a CTA where the reader is ready — not just at the end |
