# Lead Generation

You help users build systems for attracting and capturing qualified leads — people who are likely to become clients or customers. This skill covers lead magnets, opt-in strategy, landing pages, partnerships, and referral systems. The goal is not just volume — it's a steady flow of the right people at the right stage of readiness.

Works for any business that needs a pipeline: service providers, consultants, local businesses, e-commerce, SaaS, creators, and agencies.

---

## Before You Start

### Check for Context

Look for a **Business Context** document in the project knowledge files.

**If it exists:** Read it before making any recommendations. The ideal customer profile, primary trigger, anti-audience, current marketing channels, what's working, what's not, and the biggest marketing challenge are all there. Lead generation recommendations that ignore what's already working waste effort.

**If it doesn't exist:** Say — "To build a lead generation approach that fits your business, I need to understand who you're trying to attract and what you currently have in place. Do you have a Business Context document in this project? If not, tell me: who is your ideal client, how do people currently find you, and what does a new lead look like for your business?"

At minimum you need: who the ideal lead is, how leads currently come in (if at all), and what the conversion action is (book a call, fill a form, buy, sign up).

## Workflow

### Step 1: Identify the Task

Lead generation is broad. Get specific about what's needed.

**What are we working on?**
- Lead generation audit (what's the current situation, where are the gaps)
- Lead magnet (create something worth opting in for)
- Opt-in strategy (how and where to capture leads)
- Landing page for a lead gen offer
- Referral system (get existing clients to send new ones)
- Partnership strategy (get others to send leads)
- Outbound lead gen (proactively identify and reach out to potential clients)
- Full lead gen system (all of the above, prioritized)

**Clarifying questions if not obvious:**
- Where do most of your leads come from today? (Referrals, social, search, outbound, etc.)
- What does a qualified lead look like? What makes someone a good fit vs. not?
- What's the typical path from first contact to becoming a client? (How long, how many touches?)
- Is the goal more leads overall, or better-quality leads?
- Do you have an email list? A social following? Any existing audience to leverage?

**Confirm the brief:**
> "So we're building [specific lead gen asset or system] to attract [ideal lead], currently [current situation]. The goal is [outcome]. Does that sound right?"

---

### Step 2: Assess the Current Situation

Before recommending new tactics, understand what already exists. Most businesses have more lead gen assets than they realize — they're just not working together.

**Inventory check:**
- How do leads currently come in? (Referrals, SEO, social, ads, outbound, events, partnerships, none)
- Is there a lead capture mechanism on the website? (Contact form, booking link, lead magnet opt-in)
- Is there an email list? How is it currently being built?
- Is there a referral or word-of-mouth process, or does it happen passively?
- Have any outbound tactics been tried? What happened?

**Prioritization logic:**
- If the business has existing clients who are happy → **referral system first** — lowest cost, highest-quality leads
- If there's website traffic that isn't converting → **lead magnet + opt-in optimization** — capture what's already there
- If there's an engaged social audience → **audience conversion** — turn followers into leads
- If there's no inbound at all → **outbound or partnerships** — build a pipeline while longer-term channels develop
- If there's budget for ads → **paid lead gen** — fastest to traffic, requires good landing page and offer

## Lead Generation Channels

### Channel 1: Referrals

Referrals are the highest-converting, lowest-cost lead source for most service businesses. The problem is they're passive — most businesses wait for referrals to happen rather than building a system.

#### Why Referrals Don't Happen Automatically

- Clients forget to refer even when they're happy — they're busy
- They don't know exactly who to refer or what to say
- There's no prompt, trigger, or reminder to send someone your way
- They don't know you're actively looking for new clients

#### Building a Referral System

**Step 1: Identify who your best referrers could be**
- Current and past clients who got great results
- Adjacent professionals who serve the same audience (e.g., a web designer referring a copywriter; an accountant referring a financial planner)
- Former colleagues, collaborators, or mentors
- People who've recommended you informally

**Step 2: Make it easy to refer**
Give referrers the exact language to use. Most people don't refer because they're not sure how to describe what you do. Create a "referral kit" — even a short email:

```
What to say when referring [Business Name]:

"I've been working with [Name] at [Business Name] — they help [type of business]
[achieve outcome]. If you ever need [service], I'd highly recommend reaching out.
Their website is [URL] and they can be reached at [contact]."
```

**Step 3: Create a trigger to ask**
Don't wait for referrals to happen. Build ask moments into the client workflow:
- End of a project: "We loved working with you. If you know anyone who could use [service], we'd be grateful for the introduction."
- 30 days post-project: check-in email that ends with a referral ask
- After a positive moment (great review, milestone result, spontaneous compliment): "That means a lot — if you know anyone in a similar situation, we'd love an intro."

**Step 4: Follow up on referrals**
- When someone refers a lead, thank the referrer — every time, promptly
- Close the loop: let the referrer know what happened with the person they sent
- Referrers who feel appreciated refer again

**Step 5: Consider an incentive (carefully)**
Incentives work for some businesses and feel transactional in others. For professional services, a thoughtful thank-you gift often works better than a cash referral fee. For product businesses or SaaS, a discount or credit is natural. Match the incentive to the relationship.

### Channel 2: Lead Magnets

A lead magnet is something valuable enough that a prospect will trade their email address to get it. The email address starts a relationship — and a relationship can convert.

#### What Makes a Good Lead Magnet

**High-value, low-friction:** It should feel worth more than it costs (an email address). It should be immediately usable or immediately interesting.

**Specific over broad:** "The Ultimate Guide to Marketing" is too broad to feel useful. "The 5-Question Client Intake Form That Reduces Scope Creep" is specific enough to be valuable to the right person.

**Problem-aware:** It should address a problem the ideal client already knows they have — not educate them about a problem they haven't realized they have yet.

**Connected to the offer:** The best lead magnets attract people who are likely to need the paid service. A free "Website Audit Checklist" attracts people who need website work. A free "30 Easy Dinner Recipes" attracts people who cook — not necessarily people who want cooking lessons.

#### Lead Magnet Formats by Business Type

| Business Type | High-Performing Formats |
|---|---|
| Service providers (B2B) | Templates, checklists, frameworks, mini audits, short guides, calculators |
| Service providers (B2C) | Guides, quizzes, checklists, email courses, free consultations |
| Local businesses | Discount, free first visit, guide relevant to their service area |
| E-commerce | Discount code, buying guide, product quiz |
| SaaS | Free trial, freemium tier, template library, ROI calculator |
| Creators | Sample content, free chapter/lesson, resource list |
| Agencies | Audit, assessment, case study pack, proposal template |

#### Lead Magnet Formats Explained

**Checklist / template:** High-value, low-effort to consume. "The [X]-Step [Process] Checklist" or "[Role]-Ready [Document] Template." Works because it's immediately usable.

**Mini audit / self-assessment:** "Grade Your [Thing] in 10 Minutes." Works because it creates self-awareness about a problem — and positions the creator as the solution.

**Short guide / playbook:** "How to [Achieve Outcome] Without [Common Pain]." 5–10 pages. Works because it demonstrates expertise and creates enough value to justify the ask.

**Email course:** A sequence of 5–7 short emails teaching something specific. Works because it builds a relationship over time, not just a one-time download.

**Calculator or quiz:** "How Much Should You Be Charging?" or "Which [Type] Are You?" Works because interactive tools have high perceived value and are shareable.

**Free consultation / audit:** Works best for high-ticket service businesses. "30-Minute Website Review" or "Free Strategy Session." Attracts high-intent leads — only people who are genuinely interested bother booking.

#### Lead Magnet Creation Process

1. Identify the #1 question, frustration, or decision your ideal client faces before hiring you
2. Create something that helps them with that — immediately, before they become a client
3. The lead magnet should be good enough that someone could use it without hiring you — but compelling enough that it makes them want to
4. Deliver it instantly on opt-in (automated email or download page)
5. Follow it with a welcome sequence (see email-marketing skill)

### Channel 3: Opt-In Strategy

Having a lead magnet isn't enough — it needs to be placed where the right people will see it and be motivated to opt in.

#### Opt-In Placement

**Website:**
- Homepage: above the fold (for businesses where list-building is primary) or mid-page after value is established
- Blog posts / resource pages: end of each post, relevant to the content
- Exit intent popup: appears when cursor moves to close the tab — intrusive but effective for high-traffic sites
- Dedicated landing page: a standalone page with no distractions, just the opt-in

**Social media:**
- Link in bio (Instagram, TikTok, X): direct link to the opt-in landing page
- Pinned post: a post promoting the lead magnet pinned to the top of the profile
- Content that teases the lead magnet: "I made a free [thing] — link in bio"
- LinkedIn featured section: link to the landing page

**Content:**
- End of every blog post or newsletter: "Want [related thing]? Get my free [lead magnet]."
- YouTube / podcast: mention in every episode
- Guest content: reference the lead magnet when writing or appearing elsewhere

#### Opt-In Form Principles

- **One field is better than two. Two is better than five.** Every extra field reduces conversion rate. For most lead magnets, name + email is the maximum.
- **The button matters.** "Download now" beats "Submit." "Get the free checklist" beats "Sign up." First person is often stronger: "Send me the checklist."
- **Set expectations:** "You'll also receive occasional emails about [topic]. Unsubscribe anytime." Reduces unsubscribes later.
- **Deliver instantly:** Automated email sends immediately after opt-in. Don't make people wait.

#### Opt-In Landing Page Structure

For a dedicated lead magnet landing page:

```
Headline:     What they get + who it's for (the lead magnet name + the benefit)
Subheadline:  What problem it solves or what they'll be able to do
Bullets:      3–5 specific things they'll get or learn
Visual:       Mockup of the PDF, screenshot of the tool, or relevant image
Form:         Name (optional) + email + strong CTA button
Proof:        Optional — a testimonial about the lead magnet or a trust signal
No nav:       Remove site navigation — the only action on this page is to opt in
```

### Channel 4: Partnerships

Partnerships put you in front of someone else's audience — people who already trust the person introducing you.

#### Types of Lead Gen Partnerships

**Referral partnerships (reciprocal):**
Two businesses with the same audience but different services refer to each other. A brand designer and a copywriter. A business coach and a bookkeeper. A personal trainer and a nutritionist.

To make these work:
- Be specific about who you want referred ("My ideal client is a service business owner, 1–5 employees, ready to invest in their website")
- Give them the language to introduce you (same referral kit from above)
- Refer back when you can — reciprocity is what keeps partnerships alive

**Guest appearances:**
Appearing on someone else's podcast, newsletter, YouTube channel, or event. You provide value to their audience; you get exposure to people who trust that host.

To find the right opportunities:
- Identify where your ideal clients already spend their time and attention
- Look for podcasts, newsletters, or creators in adjacent niches
- Pitch a specific topic that serves their audience — not a general "come talk about my business" ask

**Content collaborations:**
Co-creating something (a guide, a webinar, a post) with a complementary business. Both audiences see the result.

**Affiliate / commission partnerships:**
A partner promotes your service in exchange for a commission on referred sales. Works well for e-commerce and SaaS; less common in professional services but not unheard of.

#### Approaching Partnership Conversations

Don't lead with "can you send me referrals." Lead with value:
- "I often have clients who need [what they do] — I'd love to refer them properly. Can we get on a call?"
- "I think our audiences overlap — would you be interested in doing [something together]?"
- Make it easy for them to say yes. The smaller the first ask, the more likely it happens.

### Channel 5: Outbound Lead Generation

When inbound isn't generating enough leads, or when you need pipeline now, outbound is how you proactively go find the right people.

#### Outbound Approaches

**Cold outreach (email or LinkedIn DM):**
Effective when personalized and targeted. See the email-marketing skill for cold email structure and the one rule that matters (specific, relevant, small ask).

For LinkedIn specifically:
- Connect request with a personalized note — not a pitch
- After connecting: engage with their content before messaging
- First message: specific, relevant to them, no pitch — start a conversation
- The goal of the first message is a reply, not a sale

**Direct outreach to warm leads:**
People who've engaged but haven't converted — visited the services page multiple times, downloaded a lead magnet but didn't book, commented on social content, opened every email. These aren't cold. Follow up with a personal, direct message.

**Event and community lead gen:**
Industry events, local business groups, online communities (Slack groups, Discord servers, Facebook groups, forums). Show up consistently, provide value, be known — don't just attend and pitch.

Rules for community lead gen:
- Give before you ask — answer questions, share useful things, be genuinely helpful
- Never pitch in a community unless it's explicitly allowed
- Let the relationship happen naturally — people hire people they know

**Content-led outbound:**
Share something specifically because it's relevant to a prospect:
"I wrote this thinking of businesses like yours — thought you might find it useful." Adds value, creates a natural reason to reach out, doesn't feel like a cold pitch.

### Channel 6: Paid Lead Generation

Paid ads — when the fundamentals are in place — can accelerate every other channel. They don't fix a broken lead gen system; they amplify what already works.

**Prerequisites before spending on ads:**
- [ ] The offer is clear and specific
- [ ] There's a dedicated landing page (not the homepage)
- [ ] The landing page converts organic traffic (at least some people are opting in / booking)
- [ ] There's a follow-up sequence for new leads (email, booking reminder, nurture)
- [ ] There's budget to test and learn — at least 60–90 days of consistent spend

**Ad platforms by business type:**

| Business | Best starting platform | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Local service business | Google Search Ads + Local Service Ads | Captures people actively searching for the service |
| B2B service provider | LinkedIn Ads | Targeting by job title, company size, industry |
| Visual/creative business | Meta (Instagram/Facebook) | Visual format, strong interest targeting |
| E-commerce | Meta + Google Shopping | Purchase intent + visual product ads |
| SaaS | Google Search + LinkedIn | Search intent + professional targeting |
| Creator / educator | Meta + YouTube | Broad reach, visual and video formats |

**The fastest path to paid lead gen that works:**
1. Start with retargeting (people who've already visited the site) — highest conversion rates, lowest cost
2. Layer in lookalike audiences (people who look like your best customers)
3. Then expand to cold audiences with a proven offer and landing page

### Step 3: Build the Lead Gen System

For users who want more than one tactic — a connected system — map how the pieces work together.

**The basic lead gen flow:**
```
Awareness            → Capture              → Nurture             → Convert
(How they find you)    (How you get their     (How you build the    (How they become
                        contact info)          relationship)          a client/customer)

SEO                  → Lead magnet opt-in   → Welcome sequence   → Book a call
Social content       → Contact form         → Nurture emails     → Purchase
Referral             → Direct booking       → Follow-up email    → Proposal / close
Paid ad              → Landing page         → Retargeting        → Book a call
Partnership          → Intro email          → Nurture            → Discovery call
```

Identify the gaps: where does the current flow break? Usually it's at one of these points:
- Not enough people entering the top (awareness problem)
- People entering but not being captured (opt-in / offer problem)
- Captured but going cold (nurture problem)
- Warm leads not converting (offer, price, or timing problem)

### Step 4: Self-Review

Before presenting any lead gen recommendations:

**Fit check:**
- Are these recommendations appropriate for the business type and stage?
- A solo freelancer doesn't need a complex paid ads funnel. A scaling SaaS doesn't need to rely on handshake referrals.
- Do the tactics match where the business already has traction?

**Specificity check:**
- Is every recommendation specific enough to act on?
- "Create a lead magnet" is not a recommendation. "Create a one-page checklist for [specific audience] addressing [specific problem]" is.

**Prioritization check:**
- Is there a clear starting point?
- Are quick wins (referral asks, opt-in on existing traffic) prioritized over long-build tactics (content SEO, partnerships)?

**Reality check:**
- How much time does the business owner have to execute?
- Are the recommendations realistic for their team size and resources?
- Would a reasonable person actually implement this, or is it too complex?

## Lead Gen by Business Stage

### Early stage (0–5 clients, building from scratch)
**Priority: direct outreach + referrals + one clear offer**
- Start with people you know — past colleagues, your network, former employers
- Do free or reduced-rate work for the right clients to build case studies
- Ask every early client for a referral and a testimonial
- Don't build a complex funnel yet — you don't know what converts

### Growing (consistent clients, want more)
**Priority: systematize referrals + build one lead magnet + optimize the website**
- Put the referral system in writing — make it a process, not a hope
- Create one lead magnet that attracts exactly the right person
- Make sure the website has a clear path to inquiry
- Start an email list even if small

### Scaling (consistent pipeline, want to reduce dependence on any one source)
**Priority: diversify channels + add paid + build content engine**
- Don't rely on any single channel for more than 40% of leads
- Invest in content that compounds over time (SEO, email list)
- Test paid ads once the fundamentals are solid
- Build partnership relationships intentionally

## Common Lead Generation Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Waiting for referrals instead of asking for them | Build a system with explicit ask moments |
| Lead magnet that's too broad ("free marketing guide") | Niche it down to a specific problem for a specific person |
| Lead magnet delivered manually instead of automatically | Set up automated email delivery — don't make people wait |
| Opt-in form buried at the bottom of the about page | Place it where traffic already exists: homepage, blog posts, social bio |
| No follow-up after capturing a lead | Every opt-in should trigger an automated welcome sequence |
| Outbound that leads with the pitch | Start with value or relevance — earn the conversation before asking for anything |
| Partnerships that are only talked about, never activated | Give partners the exact language and a specific ask |
| Running ads to the homepage | Ads need a dedicated, distraction-free landing page |
| Optimizing for lead volume instead of lead quality | Qualify in the lead magnet, opt-in form, or landing page copy |
| Building multiple channels at once | Pick one, make it work, then add the next |

**What it shows:**
- An overall system completeness indicator (how many of the four funnel stages have at least one active channel) and a count of available quick-win actions
- Four funnel stage columns (Awareness / Capture / Nurture / Convert) with channel cards showing each channel's status: Active, Build Next, or Not Started
